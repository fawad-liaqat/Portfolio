# Where do digital payments actually beat cash?

Digital payments are not useful everywhere at once. They win in particular jobs, when the conditions are right. Here are six jobs, one real example each, and where Pakistan stands.

**1. Government payments to families.**
Brazil paid pandemic aid into free digital accounts and reached more than 100 million people in nine months [S1]. Pakistan is moving the same way: BISP, which serves over 10 million people, mostly women, is shifting to digital wallets [S3].

**2. Paying at the shop.**
In India, most UPI payments go to shops, and most are under 500 rupees [S5]. In Pakistan, a government subsidy helped push shop payments on Raast from about 4 million a month to 86 million [S3]. The subsidy has ended, so the real test is now.

**3. Money from family abroad.**
About 92 in 100 remittances to Pakistan now land straight in an account or wallet [S3]. This one already works.

**4. Bills.**
In Kenya, almost everyone who pays a utility bill pays it digitally [S1]. In Pakistan, 89 in 100 bill payments and phone top ups through banks and wallets are digital [S2], though many households still pay cash at a shop.

**5. Farms and markets.**
In Kenya, 71 in 100 people paid for farm produce get the money digitally [S1]. In Pakistan, Eid cattle markets took about PKR 34 billion through QR codes and new accounts in 2026 [S2].

Everyday example: a farmer sells a goat and the money lands in his wallet, instead of a thick wad of notes he must carry home.

**6. School and hospital fees.**
In Côte d'Ivoire, the education ministry moved school fees to mobile money. Within a few years almost all fees were paid that way, and theft and lost payments fell sharply [S7]. Pakistan has QR codes at some schools and hospitals, and NADRA offices now take only QR payments [S3].

## What do the successes share?

- Someone big sets the rule, like a ministry or the government.
- The payment repeats, like a monthly bill or a school term.
- Cash is risky or costly.
- The person paying is not charged extra.

Shop payments rarely have all four, which is why they are the hardest habit to change.

## The series in one line

Pakistan has built the rails. The next step is helping more people get on them, and giving them good reasons to stay.
