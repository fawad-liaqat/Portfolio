# Method and decisions: Part 4

## Scope

Part 4 is a casebook. For six verticals (government to person transfers, merchant payments, remittances, utility and bill payments, agriculture or supply chain, education or health fees) it gives one documented case where digital payments beat cash, the evidence of value, the conditions behind it, and what it would take in Pakistan. It ends with a synthesis of the series.

Evidence cut off: 18 November 2026.

## How I chose the cases

1. A case needed a primary or official source documenting adoption. I graded the evidence of benefit: documented (measured for the case), general (stated by the source as a general finding), or none found.
2. I searched the main primary reports first: Findex 2025, the SBP Payment Systems Review FY26, the Governor's Annual Report 2025-26, the World Bank regional assessment, and the central bank reports for Brazil and Egypt. I added one other source where none of these covered the vertical: GSMA's case study on school fees in Côte d'Ivoire.
3. I did not limit countries to the Part 1 comparators. A casebook needs the clearest documented case for each job, and Findex singles out Kenya for utility and agricultural payments.
4. Where Pakistan itself had the strongest documented case (remittances), I made it the lead case.

## Rules of evidence

Every figure has a source id and a printed page number for reports. Each source is labelled PRIMARY, SECONDARY or NOT FOUND. My own calculations are marked, and nothing is filled from memory. I cite the Côte d'Ivoire case study without page numbers because I could not confirm them. The synthesis describes Part 3's question only and makes no factual claim from it.

## Decision log

| # | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | I used Brazil's emergency aid for government to person transfers | Findex documents its reach and speed (S1, p. 260), and Pakistan's move of BISP to wallets makes it directly relevant |
| 2 | I used India's UPI for merchant payments but graded its benefit as none found | The official release gives use and ticket size, not merchant cost savings |
| 3 | I made Pakistan the lead remittance case | 92% of home remittances credited to accounts or wallets (S3, p. 55) is a stronger documented result than any comparator I found |
| 4 | I used Kenya for utilities and agriculture | Findex names Kenya for both (S1, pp. 179 and 187) |
| 5 | I used the 2015 GSMA Côte d'Ivoire case for fees | It was the only documented fee case I found with both adoption and benefit; I state its age |
| 6 | I kept Pakistan's bill payment figure separate from Kenya's | SBP counts transactions through formal channels; Findex counts adults. They are not comparable |
| 7 | I treat durable merchant use after the subsidy as an open test | The subsidy ran from 1 Sep 2025 to 30 Jun 2026 (S3, p. 58); on 13 Nov 2026 the government said it would continue it, with no terms published (S9); I found no later data |
| 8 | I derived four common conditions from the cases | Each condition rests on at least two cited cases |
| 9 | I added a short customer view of the four conditions | Inclusion, experience and protection each follow from a cited case |
| 10 | I describe the Côte d'Ivoire time saving as queuing time for families | The case study supports saved queuing time; the trips it says were saved were made by schools |
