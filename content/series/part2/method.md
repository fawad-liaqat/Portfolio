# Method and decisions: Part 2

## Scope

Part 2 asks who is outside Pakistan's financial system and why, then why cash persists for people already inside. It tests financial literacy as one hypothesis among several. It draws lessons from India and Brazil, the two benchmarks chosen in Part 1, and reads them against Pakistan's demographics. Egypt, the Part 1 peer, appears only through Findex where useful. It hands off to Part 3 on open banking and the consumer.

Evidence cut off: 18 November 2026.

## Sources and how I chose them

- **Global Findex 2025 (S1)** is the spine for access and use, because it measures adults the same way in every country. Its annex table prints one Pakistan row (account, digital payment, saving, borrowing, phone, internet). Pakistan breakdowns by gender, labour force, income and use come from the report's text. Several breakdowns exist only for South Asia as a region, and the brief says so each time.
- **State Bank of Pakistan sources (S2, S11, S15)** give the administrative view: payments, the merchant subsidy, policy measures, SBP's own inclusion rate and index, and the financial literacy programmes. I prefer the Governor's Annual Report 2025-26 (S11) for FY26 policy status and the Payment Systems Review (S2) for payment tables, and I do not mix their figures in one sentence.
- **Karandaaz Financial Inclusion Survey 2024 (S12)** is the Pakistan detail source. Its narrative report includes a direct financial literacy measure and index.
- **Census 2023 (S6)** gives literacy by sex and area.
- **GSMA 2025 (S5)** gives barriers to mobile internet. I use only the top ranked barriers from its chart.
- **India (S8, S9, S14) and Brazil (S7, S1)** come from official releases and the central bank's annual report.

## How I tested the financial literacy hypothesis

The Karandaaz report gives a direct financial literacy measure by inclusion status, so I can read the association directly. It does not test cause, and I had no microdata, so my test is a weight of evidence check, not a statistical one. I sorted each piece of evidence into: supports a literacy role, points to another cause, or cannot settle. I keep basic literacy, digital skills and financial literacy apart, because the sources measure them separately.

## Rules of evidence

Every number has a source id and a printed page number for reports. Each source is labelled PRIMARY, SECONDARY or NOT FOUND. My own calculations are marked, with inputs cited. I fill no gap from memory.
