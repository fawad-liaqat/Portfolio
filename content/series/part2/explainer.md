# Why do so many Pakistanis still use cash?

Raast, Pakistan's instant payment system, moved about 2.76 billion payments last year [S2]. So why does most money still move in cash?

**First, many people are not inside the system at all.**
Only 27 in 100 Pakistani adults have a financial account, says a World Bank survey [S1]. The State Bank reports a much higher 69 in 100, using its own data and definition [S11]. Women are furthest behind. A Karandaaz survey found 14 in 100 women included, against 56 in 100 men [S12].

Everyday example: a woman who shares her husband's phone and SIM has no easy way to hold a wallet of her own. Fewer than half of Pakistani women own a SIM card [S12].

**Second, money is tight.**
Asked why they have no bank account, most people in South Asia say they do not have enough money. Fees come next [S1].

**Third, family and phones.**
Among Pakistani women without a phone, 40 in 100 say their family does not approve [S1].

**Fourth, for people already inside, digital is used but thinly.**
Most Pakistanis who have an account have made a digital payment [S1]. But far more digital payments are transfers between people than payments to shops [S2]. Shops have 3.8 million QR codes, yet each one is scanned only about once or twice a week on average (my calculation from State Bank figures) [S2].

## Is it about financial literacy?

It matters, but it is not the only thing. In the 2023 census, 53 in 100 women could read, against 68 in 100 men [S6]. Difficulty reading and writing is the top reason Pakistanis give for not using mobile internet [S5].

A Karandaaz survey also tested money skills directly, with questions on inflation, interest and risk. Only 12 in 100 women passed, against 32 in 100 men. People with an account did much better than people without one [S12]. That is a strong link, but not proof of cause: having an account may also teach people.

Money, fees, family permission and owning a SIM matter as much [S1] [S12]. So literacy is an important piece of the puzzle, not the whole answer.

## What has worked elsewhere?

India opened hundreds of millions of basic bank accounts, more than half of them for women [S9]. Brazil paid emergency aid into digital accounts and reached over 100 million people in nine months [S1]. Pakistan has a strong base to build on too: about 9 in 10 women hold an ID card [S3].
