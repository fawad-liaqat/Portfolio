# Why do so many Pakistanis still use cash?

Pakistan has a fast, modern payment system. Raast moved about 2.76 billion payments last year [S2]. So why does most money still move in cash?

**First, many people are not inside the system at all.**
Only 27 in 100 Pakistani adults have a financial account, in the World Bank's latest survey [S1]. The State Bank reports a much higher 69 in 100, using its own data and definition [S11]. Women are furthest behind. A Karandaaz survey found 14 in 100 women included, against 56 in 100 men [S12].

Everyday example: to open a mobile wallet you need a SIM card in your own name. Fewer than half of Pakistani women have one [S12]. No SIM, no wallet.

**Second, money is tight.**
When people without a bank account in South Asia are asked why, the most common answer is "I do not have enough money". Fees come next [S1].

**Third, family and phones.**
Among Pakistani women without a phone, 40 in 100 say their family does not approve [S1].

**Fourth, for people already inside, digital is used but thinly.**
Most Pakistanis who have an account have made a digital payment [S1]. But they use it for transfers and bills more than for shopping. Shops have 3.8 million QR codes, yet each one is scanned only about once or twice a week on average (my calculation from State Bank figures) [S2].

Everyday example: a shopkeeper has a QR sticker on the counter, but customers pay in notes because that is what everyone else does, and the shopkeeper needs cash for his own supplier.

## Is it about financial literacy?

Partly, maybe. Reading and writing difficulty is the top reason Pakistani adults who know about mobile internet give for not using it [S5]. Women without a bank account in South Asia are much more likely than men to say they would need help using one [S1].

But money, fees, family permission and phone ownership matter at least as much [S1]. And no source I read measures financial literacy in Pakistan directly. So literacy is one piece of the puzzle, not the whole answer.

## What has worked elsewhere?

India opened hundreds of millions of basic bank accounts, more than half of them for women [S9]. Brazil paid emergency aid into digital accounts and reached over 100 million people in nine months [S1]. Both started from a strong ID base, which Pakistan also has.
