# Method and decisions for Part 3

## Scope

Part 3 reads the State Bank's draft Open Banking Regulations 2026 [S1] as a consumer product: what a customer is promised, what she must understand, and who must work together to reach people outside the system. I read it through three lenses: customer protection, customer experience and financial inclusion. I compare the draft with the UK, Brazil and India on five dimensions. I do not assess technical API standards, security architecture, business models or AML rules, and I do not predict uptake.

Evidence cut off: 17 November 2026.

## Why these comparators

- **United Kingdom:** the lead case. Open banking began with a competition authority order on the largest banks and a standards body [S10] [S11], and it publishes usage figures [S8] [S9].
- **Brazil:** a central bank led model, the closest institutional match to SBP [S7].
- **India:** a licensed aggregator model for consented data sharing, at large scale [S14] [S15].

## How I chose the five dimensions

They are the questions a consumer and a regulator both need answered: who made it compulsory, where the data flows, how consent works, who pays when something goes wrong, and how many people use it. Each maps to a section of the draft (s. 4 and 5.1; s. 5.4; s. 7; s. 8; and the sandbox status).

## Rules of evidence

- Every number, date and status has a source marker. Reports are cited by printed page, and the draft by section and page.
- Primary means the issuing body's own document or data. Secondary means reporting or analysis about it. Where I found nothing, the cell says NOT FOUND rather than being filled from memory.
- Statements such as "the draft does not mention gender, language or basic phones" come from my own search of the draft text and are labelled as my reading.
- Suggestions for the consultation are labelled as mine.
